I've been building a set of guiding principles on my whiteboard at home and in the notes app on my phone for a few years. The world's been a bit crazy both on a macro (look around) and micro (my son's turning 1, life moves on, AI rocks my career), so I've found myself thinking about these things pretty constantly in the last few months.

These have evolved a lot over the years, but the core idea and the original question came from my uncle when I was probably around 19. He said that as long as I am always working on tougher things than I was 6 months ago, I'll be okay. At the time, I took that to heart and used it as a guiding principle. Over the years, I've added on to the list as I got older and, hopefully, wiser.

I've never shared these before, but I brought them up to someone recently, and they seemed to really like them. With that in mind, I thought I would share them more publicly.&#x20;

## My guiding principles

### Am I working on harder problems than I was six months ago?

This one was the original principle I was given. This question aims to set you on a trajectory for consistent growth. Personally, I don't feel great about being in a place where I am constantly stuck in monotony.&#x20;

This question reminds me to find challenges in my day-to-day. If there are truly none to be found, then it motivates me to move on to greener pastures or build new ways to be challenged!&#x20;

Recently, this has looked like learning piano, working to be a better dad to my son, or getting the ball rolling on tackling significant and systemic technical problems at work without being asked to do so. &#x20;

### Am I building things and skills that compound over time?

The most satisfying experiences I've had, both professionally and personally, aren't just one-and-dones. They're iterative, evolving, and often end up building momentum in my life somehow.&#x20;

My wedding was one of the greatest days of my life, but it's just a point in time in the relationship with my wife; another fantastic point on that string is the birth of my son, all the way down to last week when my wife and I witness my son saying "Meow" (pronounced as "Boww") for the first time.&#x20;

Those relationships with my wife, son, family, and friends are living and compounding things. The more you invest in the people, things you build, and skills you develop, the better your outcomes.

### Am I around people who push me to be better? If I can push them to be better, do I?

Humans are social creatures, and it's easy to be around people who are satisfied with the way of the world. I won't even say that's a bad thing; I won't put my beliefs onto others. That said, I have found that when I spend time with people who are unmotivated to improve their lot in life or fix things that could be better, I get pulled into that nihilistic direction myself.

Inversely, the same thing happens in reverse when you spend time around people who hold you accountable and hold a higher standard for themselves and you. If you surround yourself with people who push you, you will become better. Improvement is a function of effort + time. In this case, improvement is a function of effort(support) + time.&#x20;

In return, I think it's only fair to hold people around you to those same standards and help them whenever you can. So many people have done so much to help me throughout my life, it's only fair I pay that back through others if I can. &#x20;

### Am I excited to get out of bed in the morning? Are the sheets light?

I actually added this one to the list recently. I got it from my dad when talking about a big personal decision I'm in the process of making. I thought it was a really strong daily check-in. Even more so than the other questions in my list. Every morning when I get up, is it easy to do so? Am I excited for the day? If the answer is no for 4-5 days, that's okay, but more than a month? more than a few months? It's time to identify the problem and make some ruthless changes to make the sheets light again.  &#x20;

## Guiding principles over long-term goals&#x20;

As I've achieved goals, I often realized that while they're contextually useful as a driving force, they often times leave me feeling relatively hollow and meaningless when completed. The other side of the coin is that you'd set an actionable goal, and then 6 months in, that goal would become irrelevant, or it would become obvious that it was sending you in the wrong direction.&#x20;

The big ones that come to mind are finishing university or making N number of dollars per year. Immediately after completing those goals you feel great, then you sober up and realize that you were so focused on getting there you didn't really live in the day-to-day why, or maybe you're not even sure if that goal was serving you anymore or it was just something you wanted to do 5 years ago.&#x20;

This is why I really like guiding principles. They're short term, they're actionable, and all of them focus on the principles of happiness, satisfaction, and kindness to those around you. If you live those things on the day to day, and pair them with some medium-term and achievable goals (I want to ship this thing in the next 3 months) you'll be much better served and I think much happier on the journey which is really all life is at the end of the day.&#x20;
