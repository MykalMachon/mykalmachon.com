I started working at Railway!

*Well, since April 20th, technically. I've just been too busy having fun to write about it.*

## Why I Joined Railway

If you've even briefly talked with me about software, you'll know I've been a Railway fan for years. **Beyond just loving the product, a huge part of why I took the role is how Railway thinks about support.** Railway treats support as a core signal for product work.

> "Support can't be a bolt-on or an afterthought. Good support is necessary for good product because they're the same thing."
> - *myself, rambling about this to you in the past*

Railway is one of the few places I've seen actually own this ethos.

I get to talk directly to users, spot recurring friction, and help turn those patterns into product improvements. To me, support is the product’s exception handling: every thread is signal about where the product could be clearer, smoother, or more resilient.

Railway has written about this exact philosophy too: [Support Engineering Is Engineering](https://blog.railway.com/p/support-engineering-is-engineering).

## What I've Been Working On

I'm on week 8 of my onboarding and have been having a blast.
The team has been incredibly kind and generous with their time, and I feel privileged to have the opportunity to build alongside everyone.

So far, the majority of my work has been on improving our workflows around abuse detection, mitigation, and resolution. I won't get into the specifics, but it has been a fantastic opportunity to learn and grow as an engineer in the context of devtooling, security, and automation.

I've also been working on docs and QA for our [sandboxes](https://docs.railway.com/sandboxes) primitive, which has been a ton of fun.
